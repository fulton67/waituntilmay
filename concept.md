# Journey to the Center of the Internet

## Concept

How can we encode a completely separate narrative inside someone else's words, through intertextuality with another idea? The other idea is Baudrillard: the simulacrum.

Andrew Blum argues the internet is real because it runs through real tubes: physical cables going under the water. But the internet is a fictional space, and it's held inside those real things. Water carries its own meaning. Biblically, you go through the water and come out in another dimension. So the internet stores one dimension inside another dimension. That's where the simulacrum is created.

The piece holds that idea inside Blum's 54-minute talk. Out of the thousands of words he says, every word is curated so that by the end, the viewer understands the internet as a dimension inside a dimension, a copy with no original.

## How it plays

- Every version is 30 seconds. When it ends, it resets and a new version plays: the same material, a different internet.
- The distortion (datamosh) gets more intense as the 30 seconds go on.
- Certain concepts trigger distortion. When he says "internet," the image breaks down fast.
- The four phases of the image are depth: each version descends from the surface (phase 1, the image reflects reality) toward the bottom (phase 4, pure simulacrum).
- It lives at waituntilmay.com/journey-to-the-center-of-the-internet.
