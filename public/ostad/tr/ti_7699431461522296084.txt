This is what a laser does to a tattoo. Take a look at the ink. It sits below the surface in the dermis, held inside immune cells that cannot break it down. The laser fires a pulse lasting only billions of a second. The ink absorbs the light, heats faster than it can cool, and shatters into fragments. The white frosting is gas released in that instant. Now the pieces are small enough to move. Over the following weeks, immune cells carry them away through the lymphatic system. Session by session, weeks apart, the color fades until only skin remains.
