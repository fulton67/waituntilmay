Here's an update and a brief recap of my basal cell carcinoma journey. I've had this spot on my nose for decades. My dermatologist at the time somehow missed it. I now have a new dermatologist and he did a biopsy. It's a nodular basal cell on my alar, this part of the nose right here. I was told I had two options, Mohs surgery or superficial radiation. A surgical appointment was immediately set for Mohs. But I asked to have a consultation first. I spoke to the Mohs surgeon who also does SRT, superficial radiation. I asked him for an ultrasound to see the tumor before I decided, but he declined. And in case I did choose radiation, he sent me to a different radiologist. He also suggested a plastic surgeon for reconstruction in case I do Mohs. I saw the radiologist he recommended and I asked him if I could have an ultrasound prior to treatment. So I could decide on treatment method. But he also declined saying, well, you really can't see that much. Then I had an appointment with the plastic surgeon. He showed me photos of the paramedial flap on the forehead. And he drew an example of how that paramedial flap works as we were speaking. That scared me. Then when I told the Mohs surgeon I needed more time to think about it, he sent out a letter saying I had declined treatment, which wasn't so. After that, I found a facility on my own which does high frequency ultrasounds, HFUS. Thankfully, my dermatologist agreed to order one for me. He also set up an appointment with a different Mohs surgeon for a second opinion. I now have the shape and measurements of the tumor and most importantly, it doesn't attach to the cartilage. And since I wasn't allowed to take a photograph of the ultrasound image, I drew a picture of it from memory. This is the top layer of the skin. This is the tumor.
