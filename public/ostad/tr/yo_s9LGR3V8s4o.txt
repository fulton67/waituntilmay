Buccal fat removal is becoming a very popular surgery for people who are looking to take out all that extra chubbiness in their cheeks and to slim and taper the lower 2/3 of their face. So, what is buccal fat? Who's a good candidate for surgery, and how do we take it out? The buccal fat pad is one of the deep fat pads of the face, meaning it lies deep to your SMAS fascia and the muscles that control facial movement. Now, the fat pad itself is actually quite large with its anterior lobe sitting in the buccal space of your cheek, and that's a part of your dressing surgery, extending up underneath your bony zygomatic arch, which defines the height of your cheekbone, and going all the way up into your temporal fossa, lying below your temporalis muscle. Now, as a deep fat pad, buccal fat does not respond to weight gain or weight loss like superficial fat on the rest of your body. Instead, it slowly grows in size between the ages of 10 and 20, and then after your teenage years, it progressively starts to atrophy or shrink.
