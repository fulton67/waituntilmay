One of the most important things that I tell patients about injectables is the product is only one part of the result. Technique truly matters. Two physicians can use the same product on the same area of the face and approach that treatment very differently. Because every face is different. Before I inject anything, I'm studying the patient's anatomy. I'm also thinking about what's underneath the surface. With a neural modulator, really technique begins with understanding facial and muscle anatomy and movement. It's really not simply about seeing a line and injecting it. I want to understand which muscle is creating that movement, how strong that muscle is, how it interacts with the surrounding muscles. The placement of an injection, the dose, the patient's individual anatomy, truly all influence the treatment. My goal is never to eliminate expression. It's to create balance while allowing the patient to continue looking like themselves. With hyaluronic acid injectables, we're thinking a bit differently. Now placement and depth becomes incredibly important. The face is really a three-dimensional structure and filler can really behave differently depending on where and how it's placed. I need to understand not only where I'm injecting, but the anatomical plane I'm working in and the structures around it. That understanding is important for both the aesthetic result and patient safety. Technique also means knowing how much product to use. More isn't necessarily better. Sometimes a very small amount placed thoughtfully can create a meaningful improvement. Sometimes the best decision is really not to inject a particular area at all. After more than three decades of working with facial anatomy, both surgically and aesthetically, one principle that has remained very consistent for me is that I have to respect the anatomy. The face isn't a blank canvas. It's a complex dynamic structure and every treatment has to work within that structure. So when injectable treatments are performed thoughtfully, the goal shouldn't be for someone to look at you and immediately know what you've had done. They should simply see you as balanced, refreshed, natural. So the product choice matters, but really understanding where to place it, how to place it, and how much to use. And when not to use it is really where the technique becomes an art.
