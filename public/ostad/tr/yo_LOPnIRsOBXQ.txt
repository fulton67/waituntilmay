When we talk about advances in injectable fillers, we tend to focus on what happens when the product is injected. But something that's equally important that happens long before the filler ever reaches the patient, it really happens during the manufacturing process. Evelise is a collection of hyaluronic acid injectables made using something called cold-ex technology. That temperature actually plays in preserving the hyaluronic acid molecule. Hyaluronic acid, otherwise known as HA, is a molecule that occurs naturally within our skin and all our tissues, connective tissue. And one of its important properties is its ability to bind water. But hyaluronic acid is also sensitive to the way it's processed. This is where the key important thing is. Traditional filler manufacturing can involve heat during the cross-linking process and heat can contribute to the breakdown or sort of what we call fragmentation of that hyaluronic acid chains. Cold-ex technology takes really a different approach. It uses near freezing temperature during the manufacturing process. The goal is a filler that's designed to perform better within the treatment area while looking and feeling natural. And within the Evelise collection, we can currently have Evelise form, which has a little more structure. Evelise smooth, which is therefore smoother, and both are made using cold-ex technology. And they have really different, what we call viscoelastic properties, different viscosities. And this way allows us to select a product that's based on the treatment goal. And for me, as a physician and an artist, this is where science really becomes clinically relevant. We're not simply looking at how much volume a filler can create. We're thinking more about the properties of the gel, how it behaves within a dynamic area of the face, and ultimately how that translates into the result we see and feel. Patients today are increasingly telling me the same thing. They want natural results. They don't want to look like they've had filler. They want to remain and maintain their expression. They want to maintain their individuality. They don't want to look different. And that's what makes the science behind cold-ex technology really interesting to me and useful. So it's an innovation to me that really begins at the molecular and manufacturing level. It's designed around something very simple, a natural-looking and a natural-feeling result.
