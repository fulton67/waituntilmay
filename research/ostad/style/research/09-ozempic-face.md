## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **It tracks how much you lose, not which drug. In a 2026 survey of 1,226 patients, half of those who lost more than 20% of their body weight noticed lost facial volume, against 13% of those who lost under 10%.** Dermatol Ther 2026. [PMID 42579223](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42579223/). It backs his opening line with data; it is a self-report survey, so say “patients reported”.
2. **In the main semaglutide trial, people lost about 15% of their body weight on average, roughly 15 kg.** STEP 1. [PMID 33567185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185/). It explains why the face changes so fast.
3. **Across GLP-1 trials, roughly a quarter to a third of the weight lost is lean tissue, not fat.** Aesthet Surg J Open Forum 2026 narrative review (~25–30%). [PMID 42820120](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42820120/). Interesting but drifts from the face.

**Chosen: #1.**

## 4. Do not say on camera

- “Ozempic for weight loss” as if approved for it (see naming note).
- Anything that sounds like advice to stop or change a GLP-1 medicine. Defer to the prescriber.
- “Ozempic damages your skin/collagen.” This is not established, and it contradicts his own thesis.
- “Sculptra is FDA-approved for temples / for Ozempic face.” Temple use is off-label; no product carries an “Ozempic face” indication.
- Comments on any celebrity's face or weight (privacy and defamation risk; it also undercuts the clinical tone).
- Promises about fat-graft survival while the patient is on a GLP-1 (#7).
