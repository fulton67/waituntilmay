## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **Standard lab sections, cut every 4 mm, caught cancer left at the edge only 44% of the time. Mohs looks at the whole edge.** Kimyai-Asadi 2005. [PMID 16112355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16112355/). It explains *why* Mohs in one number. (The study used small facial BCCs excised with 2 mm margins; say “in a study of small facial basal cells”.)
2. **A basal cell usually reaches past what you can see. Standard surgery needs about 4 mm of normal-looking skin to clear 95% of them.** Wolf & Zitelli 1987. [PMID 3813602](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3813602/). It supports “watch how little we take”.
3. **More than half the recurrences of high-risk facial basal cells appeared after year five.** van Loo 2014 (56%). [PMID 25262378](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25262378/). A good line for “keep coming for skin checks”.

**Chosen: #1.**

## 4. Do not say on camera

- “100% cure”, or a bare 99% without “first-time basal cell, five years”.
- “Mohs is best for every skin cancer.” The AUC rates Mohs inappropriate for some scenarios (e.g., small primary superficial BCC on the trunk).
- “No scar.”
- Disparaging general or plastic surgeons who excise. Keep the comparison to the evidence.
- Gore for its own sake (platform policies). Keep the specimen and defect shots brief, and give a warning card if needed.
