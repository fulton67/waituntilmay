## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **Researchers who need to create dark spots on purpose for a study use 30% TCA: the same strength sold for home use.** Br J Dermatol 2022. [PMID 34625951](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34625951/). (The study applied it to small spots on the trunk in a controlled setting; don't overstate it as “on faces”.)
2. **Going from 20% to 30% TCA made the dark marks measurably darker at every time point.** Same study. It answers “isn't a little stronger fine?”
3. **A 2026 review found only 7 studies reporting peel outcomes in Fitzpatrick IV–VI skin, out of 473 screened, and one of the 7 was about chemical burns from improper use.** Cureus 2026. [PMID 42306365](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42306365/)

**Chosen: #1.**

## 4. Do not say on camera

- “I use the Monk scale, which is better than Fitzpatrick” as a medical fact. It is his practice choice, and it is not validated for treatment selection (#5).
- “It will always fade.” Dermal PIH can persist (#7).
- Hydroquinone as something to buy over the counter. Since the CARES Act (Sept 2020), OTC hydroquinone skin lighteners are unapproved new drugs, and FDA sent 12 warning letters in April 2022 ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-works-protect-consumers-potentially-harmful-otc-skin-lightening-products)).
- Any claim that LUMA Cream “fixes” or “treats” PIH. It is a compounded, non-FDA-approved product (site lists arbutin, Retin-A, antioxidants, AHA); a retinoid or AHA on freshly injured skin can itself irritate. Present it, if at all, as “what I may prescribe once the skin has calmed down”.
- Naming or showing a specific at-home peel brand.
