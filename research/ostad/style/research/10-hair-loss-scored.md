## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **Biotin can make a heart-attack blood test read falsely normal. The FDA has warned about it since 2017.** FDA safety communication (2017, updated 2019) on biotin interference with lab tests, including falsely low troponin. [FDA: biotin interference with troponin tests](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/biotin-interference-troponin-lab-tests-assays-subject-biotin-interference). The lowest score with a real-world sting; it also signals the honesty the slot depends on.
2. **When PRP went head-to-head with minoxidil, the hair-density difference wasn't significant.** Aesthetic Plast Surg 2026 (9 RCTs). [PMID 41219547](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41219547/). Honest in *both* directions: PRP isn't worse, but $20 a month did as well on density.
3. **In a 23-study analysis, finasteride beat topical minoxidil at a year.** Gupta 2022. [PMID 35107565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35107565/). It fixes the omission in #9.

**Chosen: #1.**

## 4. Do not say on camera

- That exosomes “regrow hair” or are FDA-approved or cleared. FDA says no exosome products are approved, and exosome products intended to treat disease require approval (2019 notification; 2020 consumer alert: [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/consumers-biologics/consumer-alert-regenerative-medicine-products-including-stem-cells-and-exosomes)). He sells exosomes for hair, so the video must state plainly that they are unapproved and early, and disclose that he sells them (the plan's “including the ones I sell” does this).
- That PRP is an FDA-approved hair-loss treatment. It has no FDA hair-loss indication; present it as an in-office procedure with limited evidence.
- That low-dose oral minoxidil is approved for hair loss. It is off-label (oral minoxidil is approved for hypertension).
- “Every treatment sheds first” (#7).
- Before/after photos without consent, matching conditions, and a “results vary” line (FTC testimonial rules apply to a practice's own marketing).
