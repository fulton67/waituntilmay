## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **About 7 in 10 melanomas appear as a new spot, not in an old mole.** Pampena 2017, 38 studies. [PMID 28864306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28864306/). It flips the viewer's instinct (watching the old mole) and sets up the ugly-duckling payoff.
2. **Under 40, any one mole has roughly a 1-in-200,000 chance a year of turning into melanoma.** Tsao 2003. [PMID 12622618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12622618/). Reassuring and specific; it is an estimate from a model, so say “roughly”.
3. **When dermatologists compared each patient's moles with each other, they biopsied fewer moles per melanoma found.** Gaudy-Marqueste 2017. [PMID 28196213](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28196213/). Less punchy, but backs up the close.

**Chosen: #1.**

**Different angle:** make the reveal about *new versus old* (the de novo statistic) and the ugly duckling *in context*. Show each spot among the patient's other moles (a back photo), not as two isolated crops. Two isolated spots side by side actually work against the ugly-duckling message, which depends on comparing a spot with the same person's other moles.

## 4. Do not say on camera

- “I can tell from a photo” or any line suggesting he diagnoses remotely. It contradicts claim 8 and invites telehealth-diagnosis requests.
- “Dermoscopy tells me for sure.” Only histopathology confirms. Dermoscopy improves accuracy (#7) but is not diagnostic certainty.
- “Moles don't turn into melanoma.” About 29% of melanomas are nevus-associated (#1). Say “rarely”, not “never”.
- Any survival percentage without its stage (for example a quoted 99%). It is true only for localized disease.
- Patient images need written consent for social use with identifying features removed (HIPAA).
