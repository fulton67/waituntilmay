## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **The best-known long-term proof of “preventive Botox” is one pair of identical twins.** Binder 2006. [PMID 17116793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17116793/). Surprising, true, and fits the contrarian tone. He should say it fairly: the treated twin did look better.
2. **At a 10-unit “baby” dose, 83% of patients' frown lines were back by four months, against about 30% at 20 units or more.** Carruthers 2005. [PMID 15871316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15871316/). It reframes baby Botox as more visits, not less product.
3. **Botox Cosmetic is approved only for lines that are already moderate to severe.** Label §1. [Label PDF](https://www.rxabbvie.com/pdf/botox-cosmetic_pi.pdf)

**Chosen: #1** (#2 as the mid-video beat).

## 4. Do not say on camera

- “Botox prevents wrinkles” as a fact. The evidence is a case report plus mechanism studies (#3).
- “Botox is completely safe.” The label carries a boxed warning; say “very low risk at cosmetic doses”.
- Encouraging treatment of a patient with no moderate-to-severe lines. That is outside the approved indication (#2); for a video that sells Botox/Jeuveau, keep it as “it's a choice, not a medical need”.
- Treating units as interchangeable between brands. Jeuveau's label says it is not interchangeable with other botulinum toxins, and Jeuveau is approved only for glabellar lines (Evolus label, 2019: [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2019/761085Orig1s000Lbl.pdf)).
- Tying this video to the Jeuveau promotion (free gift box / prize entry). Pairing a medical-advice video with an inducement is a bad look for a “consult can end with no” message.
