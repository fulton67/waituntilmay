# 08 · Why your brows dropped after Botox

Slot: PLAN.md ### 08 (Wed 2026-11-18). First line: “If your eyebrows dropped after Botox, that was placement, not the product.”
Claim in the plan: *A heavy brow after Botox is almost always about placement, and an experienced injector can predict it before the needle goes in.*

## 2. Hook detail candidates (ranked)

1. **The official label tells injectors to treat the frown muscles *along with* the forehead to stop the brows dropping. Treating more prevents the droop.** Label §2.4. [Label PDF](https://www.rxabbvie.com/pdf/botox-cosmetic_pi.pdf). Counter-intuitive, verifiable, and it fits the “placement” thesis.
2. **Even in the FDA trials, about 1 in 50 forehead patients got a dropped brow; none did on placebo.** Label (13/665 vs 0/315). Honest and specific; it pre-empts “a good injector never causes this”.
3. **Jeuveau, which the practice uses, is FDA-approved only for frown lines.** Jeuveau label. Better used as a compliance note than as a hook.

**Chosen: #1.**

## 4. Do not say on camera

- “A good injector never causes this.” The label shows 2% even in trials (#1).
- “Not the product” in a way that implies brands are unit-for-unit equivalent (#2).
- Injecting Jeuveau into the forehead while calling it approved. Jeuveau's only approved indication is glabellar lines, so forehead use is off-label. If the filmed product is Jeuveau and the area is the forehead, either say it is off-label or film BOTOX Cosmetic, which is approved for forehead lines.
- Bacteriostatic saline, or any “dissolve your Botox” fix (#7).
- Naming an eye-drop brand as a fix for a dropped *brow*. Drops act on the eyelid muscle, not the brow.
