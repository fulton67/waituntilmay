## What he says (the cut audio, in order)

I don't really believe in filler fatigue, where patients feel that a lot of filler can actually make somebody's skin stretch, it can make somebody look overfilled, or the fillers can migrate. I find either happens from improper injections, from people who are not trained to do those injections, from doing too much filling. The underlying physiology of aging is that we lose volume. And when we lose volume, we lose fatty tissue and we lose the support structure for our facial skin. That happens from aging. And when you lose that support structure, the skin sags. And I have my own sort of methodology that I find really gives us incredible results utilizing micro-cannulas. And really, it's injecting fillers on the cheekbones and that causes a lot of lifting because the ligaments that really support our cheekbones diminish over time and as a result the skin falls. I find fillers work really great. I don't see them migrate unless you're really using the wrong filler. It may migrate in a lip that too much is injected. I have my approach of basically injecting the cheekbones, maybe minimal amounts in the laugh line, marionette lines a little bit on the lower lids to really give you mid-cheek filling. But I am not really a fan of creating golf size, you know, filling in the mid-cheek, that looks awkward. So I just want to address this issue of filler fatigue. I really think fillers are here to stay.
