## Run 2

25. **faster-whisper `small` on the GPU (RTX 3050, CUDA)**, not mlx-whisper: this is Windows, not Apple silicon. VAD is on: without it, silent videos come back as "Thank you for watching." Language is auto-detected and the transcript kept in the original language; `transcript_lang` records it (449 en, then es 26, pt 17, hi 11, ar 10…).
26. **Captions first.** TikTok's own captions (fetched in run 1) and YouTube subtitles count as `captions`; whisper runs only when there are none. 452 of 815 transcripts are captions.
27. **`no_speech` is a fourth `transcript_status`**, beyond the spec's three. 209 videos were fetched and listened to and contain no voice. Calling them `unavailable` would hide that we know what they are; calling them `whisper` would imply a transcript exists.
28. **Instagram transcripts came from the CDN `videoUrl` in `raw/`.** Run 1's scrape is from 2026-11-01 and the links still worked. 195/247 (79%) got a transcript, above the 70% bar, so `apify/instagram-reel-scraper` wasn't run. Apify spend for run 2: $0.
29. **Run 2 taxonomy additions** (the transcripts demanded them): genres `hack / tip`, `diagnosis / is-it-X`, `brand advertorial / sponsored` (separates paid influencer plugs from a clinic's own `ad / promo`), `personal journey / diary`; formats `interview / podcast clip`, `skit / acted`; hooks `visual shock / gross-out`, `credential open`, `POV / you-are-the-patient`, `promise of a result`, `list open`. `direct address` now means only a literal greeting/"today we're talking about" with no other device; that is what un-collapsed run 1's hooks (367 → 79).
30. **Nine parallel readers, one merge.** The readers wrote `classify2/out_NN.json` and a checker validated each file; one merge applied all 1,031 rows. His own 109 videos are forced to `makeable_by_ostad = true` at merge.
31. **The tone guard counts a row once for each tone it carries** (tone takes 1–2 values), which is the strict reading. It failed twice: readers had used `clinical`, then `casual-friend`, as the default for "a doctor is talking". Both times every row holding the top value was re-read in full, per the spec; no quota was given to readers.
32. **`audience_reaction` is null on most rows**: run 1 scraped comments only for 30 TikToks and a few IG posts, and run 2 scrapes nothing new. 'What the audience says back' in each theme therefore leans on those comments plus the 47 questions.
33. **18 themes, from 32 clusters.** sentence-transformers (all-mpnet-base-v2) over subject + claim + opening line, Ward clustering at k=32, then read and named by hand (`classify2/clusters.md`). Clusters that held two topics were split, and 104 videos were moved row by row (`data/theme_map.json` holds both). 18 is the top of the spec's range; merging further hid what the audience asks (e.g. skin cancer vs "what is this on my skin" behave very differently: 5.96× vs 0.96×).
34. **The Read figure gives each theme its own row on one shared log axis**, labelled with its name at its median, rather than 18 colours in one strip. 18 hues can't be told apart (dataviz rule: at most 8 categorical colours), and the theme name is the label anyway.
35. **The Him page's first figure uses views, not × usual.** His × usual is measured against his own median, so it is 1× by construction and says nothing about his reach next to the niche.
36. **Transcripts are fetched on demand** from `/ostad/tr/{id}.txt` (815 files) rather than bundled into the page JSON, so the 2.7 MB of transcript text is not in the page data (videos.json stays about 1 MB, 320 KB gzipped).
37. **Theme and plan pages were written by parallel writer agents** from computed dossiers (`classify2/dossier_*.md`); every number is copied from a dossier, every example id is checked against its theme, and the first four examples on each theme are makeable. Cares are proposed by a reader and counted/ranked by `scripts/cares.py` (frequency × performance), so the ranking is computed, not judged.
