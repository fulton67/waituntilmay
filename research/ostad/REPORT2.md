# What the skin niche is saying, and what Dr. Ostad should say back

The niche's best subject is the one he is best qualified for. Videos about whether a spot is cancer, and what Mohs will do to a face, run at a median of 6 times their creator's usual views, and 86% of them are videos he could make himself. He has three, all awareness-month talks. Below: what the 922 niche videos are actually about, what their audience asks, which tones and shapes win, what he posts instead, and the 20 videos we would make first.

## What the niche is talking about

We read 922 videos from the dermatology niche on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube, plus 109 of Dr. Ostad's own, and listened to every one that had a voice. They sort into 18 conversations. Below, each one in a paragraph, with four videos he could make himself. The figure puts every theme on one line: each dot is a video, placed by how many times its creator's usual views it got, and the tick is the theme's median.

### Is it cancer? Spots, skin checks and Mohs {#is-it-cancer}

The strongest theme in the niche for him. People want to know whether a spot will hurt them, and what Mohs will do to their face. Doctors answer with a quiz ("Is that skin cancer or not?") or a 2 mm melanoma under a dermoscope; patients answer with their own scars. 50 videos, and the median does 6 times its creator's usual, 8.5 times for the 43 he could make. His three videos here are awareness-month talks; one has 398 views.

### What is this on my skin? {#what-is-this}

A doctor names a bump, rash or patch in thirty seconds, and just as often says what it isn't: forehead bumps that are yeast, light patches that aren't vitiligo, the "scar" that was basal cell carcinoma. 40 videos, 85% makeable. The typical one lands at par, but the top six run from 84 to 201 times usual. Rosacea is the single biggest topic.

### Faces: filler, fat and Ozempic face {#faces}

The fear of looking older meets the fear of looking done. Ozempic face, buccal fat removal, filler that migrated, and Sculptra pitched as the subtle fix. 93 videos, the second-largest theme, 66% makeable, median about 1 times usual. He does filler and lower-eyelid surgery, so he can say when filler is the wrong answer, and his two best videos here already do.

### Botox, baby Botox and the frozen-face fear {#botox}

One long argument about when to start and how much is too much. Baby Botox is either sensible small doses or a phrase built to hook women in their twenties. 29 videos with a median of 2.7 times usual, 66% makeable. His Jeuveau spokesperson spots run at half to one times his usual; treating his nurse Colleen on camera got 522,373 views.

### Lifting without surgery: threads, Morpheus8, Emface {#lifting}

Can you get a lift without a facelift? Clinics say yes, then the regret videos answer: Morpheus8 that melted fat, thousands spent for nothing. 36 videos, 58% makeable, median just under 1 times usual. He sells the devices and does the surgery, so he can say who is a device patient and who needs a neck lift. Nobody here does.

### What a derm actually gets done {#anti-aging-menu}

The insider's menu: treatments rated out of ten, "what I'd never get", "I'm almost 60". 44 videos, the most makeable theme at 77%, but only 11% are winners. Nearly every "what I get done" video is a woman. A male surgeon in his 50s saying what he does to his own face is an empty lane, as long as it doesn't become an ad for his menu.

### Dark spots, melasma and skin of color {#dark-spots}

Mostly people with brown and Black skin trying to fade marks that won't leave, and afraid of clinics that don't know dark skin. Influencers sell the fix; doctors warn against peeling at home. 60 videos with a median of 2.4 times usual and 37% winning, though only 47% are makeable. His own Instagram videos on peels for skin of colour and on LUMA each have about 50,000 views; this audience already finds him.

### Acne and acne scars {#acne}

Two halves: scars, where the doctors who win separate marks that fade from dents that don't; and stubborn adult acne. 28 videos, median 1.4 times usual, 61% makeable. He does subcision, TCA cross and fat transfer himself, which no serum seller can say.

### Tretinoin, retinol and the purge {#tretinoin}

Surviving the first months on a retinoid. Patients post purge diaries; doctors win by contradicting the viewer in the first line ("It does nothing"). 39 videos, 74% makeable, median about 1 times usual. 12 of them are the same azelaic-plus-tretinoin combo. He compounds his own retinoid strengths, so "why I'd start you lower" is his line.

### Routines, the skin barrier and glass skin {#routines-barrier}

The biggest theme, 107 videos and 12% of the niche: a tug of war between more steps and fewer. Influencers add; the doctors who win take away, and roast a 20-product routine. Median 1.3 times usual, 53% makeable. He was in the Wall Street Journal on exactly this ("Is Your Antiaging Routine Making Your Skin Look Worse?"). His six videos here are silent facial clips with 279 to 1,055 views.

### The sunscreen fight {#sunscreen-fight}

An argument, not a tutorial: toxic filters and "some sun is good" against the derms. 21 videos, the smallest theme, and the ones he could make sit at 0.8 times usual. He has none. As the surgeon who removes these cancers he can win the argument, but not by selling his own $95 sunscreen inside it.

### What's worth buying {#worth-buying}

Money. "Things I'd never spend money on" lists trash La Mer and $1,000 LED masks. The losing side is the med-spa line that "medical grade" means stronger. 57 videos, 67% makeable, median 1 times usual. He compounds, so he can explain what prescription strength actually changes and admit where the drugstore is fine.

### Salmon sperm, exosomes and copper peptides {#regenerative-hype}

The Korean-import gold rush, mostly influencer plugs with discount links; only 31% makeable and 8% winning. The sceptic is the only voice that works, and he is already on record: Cosmopolitan used him as its salmon sperm source in 2025. Your own platelets versus a stranger's exosomes in a $22 serum is a line he can draw.

### Lasers and the healing diary {#lasers}

The recovery, not the laser: day three of Fraxel, the CO2 face that looks frightening but doesn't hurt. 68 videos, 56% makeable, median 1.1 times usual. He has 15 laser videos, more than any other theme, and none shows a patient's week after; most are device introductions.

### In the chair: PRP, microneedling and small procedures {#in-the-chair}

What the appointment looks like. 23 videos, weak overall at 0.74 times usual, carried by a couple of outliers. Nine of his eleven videos here are wordless vein treatment clips, seven under 2,000 views. A doctor narrating the procedure does better than footage over a pop song.

### Hair loss and hair 'botox' {#hair}

Two conversations: salon "hair botox", which has no toxin in it and takes the big numbers, and medical hair loss. 28 videos; the part he could make (46%) does 1.8 times usual. He injects real Botox daily and has two years of PRP results, so he can give the verdict his silent before-and-afters never do.

### Inside the practice {#inside-the-practice}

The day in the life, mostly young New York dermatologists. 42 videos and only 5% winners, but the two that won state the job in the first line, one of them "why I became triple board certified". That is his credential. His five EXOMIND posts here are his most-watched, at 15,730 to 33,990 views.

### The derm as entertainer {#entertainer}

The sideshow: drained cysts over pop music, lip-syncs, staff skits. 93 videos, but only 24% is anything he could make. The narrow slice is his own procedure footage explained by the surgeon, awake patients under local anaesthetic, and a dry reaction to someone else's clip. The rest would cost him the patients he wants.

## What the audience asks

We grouped what these videos answer into the questions viewers would type, then ranked them by how many videos answer each and how well those videos do. The full list of 25, each with its videos and a box for his answer, is on the Cares tab.

The two biggest are about getting started: whether to use retinol or tretinoin (47 videos, median 3 times usual), and whether anyone really needs a ten-step routine (62 videos, though those land at par). Then come dark spots that won't fade (39 videos, 3.3 times), and "does it hurt, and how bad is the downtime?" (44 videos, mostly answered by influencers).

The questions that perform best are the frightening ones. "If a mole has to come off, will it leave a scar on my face?" is answered by 29 videos at 6.7 times usual. "Is this spot skin cancer?" is 28 videos at 5.5 times. "Will my acne scars ever go away?" is 29 videos at 6 times, and "why won't my melasma go away?" is only 17 videos but runs at 10.7 times, mostly answered by influencers. He has blog posts on all four and no video that answers any of them.

Two questions he has nothing on at all: whether you can do peels and microneedling at home without hurting yourself, and whether viral ingredients like copper peptides and PDRN cream are real. Both are worries a senior surgeon can settle in under a minute.

His videos are strongest where demand is weakest. He already has good material on Botox that doesn't look frozen, restrained filler and purging; none of those made the top 25.

## Which tone and shape win

Tone matters more than topic. Confessional videos, where someone says what happened to them or what they actually do, run at a median of 3.1 times usual and 31% of them win. Among the ones he could make it is 4.3 times. Fear and warning do well when there is an answer attached (1.3 times for makeable videos). The flat clinical explainer sits at exactly 1 times, and casual-friend chat is below par at 0.87.

Contradicting the viewer is a trap on its own. Videos whose tone is contrarian sit at 1 times and only 10% win; a "contrarian claim" first line is the same. The doctors who win by contradicting do it inside a story or a case, not as a lecture.

Shape: opening on a before-and-after reveal is the strongest start in the niche (5.2 times, 34% winning). Putting the viewer in the patient's seat follows at 3.9 times. A story opening does 2.3 times for makeable videos. The weakest is opening on credentials, at 0.8 times. A doctor's voice over procedure footage does 2.2 times for makeable videos; podcast clips do 0.85, and only 3% of them win.

Who speaks matters too. Patients telling their own story run at 5.2 times. Doctors make 437 of the 922 niche videos but sit at 1 times, with 13% winning. The audience wants the doctor, but with a case, a patient or his own story in it, not a lecture.

## What Dr. Ostad does instead

His median video gets 876 views. The niche videos he could make get 57,790.

65% of his videos are salesy, against 13% of the winning videos he could make. 31% are ads or promos, against 5% of the winners. 1% are confessional, against 15%. 5% carry a warning, against 21%. Skin cancer, where he is strongest, is 3% of his videos and 12% of the winners. 34 of his 109 videos have no voice at all: a treatment clip over a pop song, or a device introduction. His most common openings are "promise of a result" and "today we're talking about", both below par.

What he does have is footage, consenting patients, and a record the niche can't match: Mohs fellowship, reconstruction, NYU teaching, the Skin Cancer Foundation, the Wall Street Journal on routines and Cosmopolitan on salmon sperm. Treating his nurse on camera got 522,373 views, his best ever. It was a scene, not an ad.

## What we make, and why

The plan is 20 videos from Monday 9 November, five a week, with 10 backups and 40 more titles. Every slot comes from a theme above and copies the shape of at least two niche videos he could make, which are linked on the Plan tab.

Every Monday is skin cancer: a two-spot "which one would I remove?" quiz, a Mohs case from first stage to closed wound, an answer to the commenter who called Mohs a scam, and the four weeks that decide a mole scar. These are the videos only he can make, in the theme where the niche's numbers are best.

Botox and dark spots fill the middle of the week, three each. On Botox he speaks from what he has seen happen to faces that started at 24, and why brows drop after Botox (placement, not the product). On dark skin he warns against home TCA peels, tells melasma apart from sun spots, and explains that a laser burns because of its setting, not because of the skin.

The rest are single bets in themes where he has a line nobody else has: Ozempic face explained by the surgeon who rebuilds faces, a hair-loss scoreboard that includes the treatments he sells, salmon sperm injected versus rubbed on, a dermatologist in his fifties showing everything on his own face, three things he won't do to his own face, and a day on Lexington Avenue.

The desk-day pieces are the confessional ones: what he does to his own face, what he has seen happen over two decades. Each one opens in his own voice, under 15 words, and none ends on a product. The 20 split into five shoot days (a desk day, a screen day with photos and the dermoscope, a procedure day, patient sit-downs and one day following him around), so the first month can be filmed in a week.
