# Tretinoin, retinol and the purge

This theme is about surviving the first months on a retinoid. Patients post day-one diaries, flaking close-ups and "trust the process" purge videos, and the doctors answer with corrections: a purge is rarer than the internet thinks and never lasts past three months, 0.1% isn't better than a low strength, start every third night and sandwich it with moisturizer, and yes, you need it for life but three nights a week holds the results. The other big thread is the viral azelaic acid in the morning, tretinoin at night combo. People watch because they're scared of looking worse before they look better.

## The numbers

39 videos, 4% of the niche. 23% of them are winners and the median one does 1.08× its creator's usual (1.00× for the ones he could make); 74% (29 videos) are videos Dr. Ostad could make himself.

## What people claim

- If you're still "purging" after three months, it isn't a purge; true purging is rare and short-lived.
- Going up to 0.1% tretinoin doesn't give better anti-aging results, only faster ones with more irritation.
- You have to keep using a retinoid to keep the results, but after a year of nightly use, three nights a week maintains them.
- Tretinoin doesn't stop working and skin doesn't build resistance; your acne, often hormonal, has outgrown what a topical can do.
- Tretinoin is a medicine, not a cosmetic: not in pregnancy or breastfeeding, and not on eczema or rosacea without a doctor.
- Azelaic acid in the morning and tretinoin at night gives the benefits without the irritation, as long as you keep it off the nose and eye corners.
- Most patients can't tolerate tretinoin, so a retinol or retinal used consistently is often the better choice.
- 0.1% and 0.05% are over-prescribed; start low, every third night, with moisturizer under and over it.
- Not everyone needs tretinoin, azelaic and glycolic together; tretinoin does most of the work.
- No viral influencer recipe gives everyone glass skin.

## How it sounds
