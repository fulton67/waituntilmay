# Inside the practice

This is the day-in-the-life genre: dermatologists, mostly young and mostly in New York, walking the viewer through an espresso, a subway ride, a cyst excision, Botox, a Mohs case, then Pilates or a podcast. The argument underneath is that dermatologists do far more than beauty: one doctor counts 38 patients, 15 skin checks and over a hundred frozen pre-cancers in eight hours. Around it sit office tours, staff skits about the medical assistant who really runs the place, salary breakdowns and residency advice. People watch for a look behind a door they never get to open, and the videos that work show the work, not the lifestyle.

## The numbers

42 videos, 5% of the niche. Only 5% of them are winners and the median one does 0.81× its creator's usual, though the ones he could make do better at 1.23×; 57% are videos Dr. Ostad could make himself. This is also where his own most-watched posts sit: five EXOMIND announcements, at 15,730 to 33,990 views each.

## What people claim

- A dermatologist's day can be 38 patients in eight hours, 15 full-body skin checks, over a hundred pre-cancers frozen, nine biopsies, a basal cell surgery, filler and Botox (80×).
- Being triple board-certified comes from wanting to treat the whole patient, not just the skin (25×).
- Dermatologists treat all hair, skin and nail disorders, and "we are not just there about the beauty."
- A Mohs surgeon's morning is four Mohs cases plus an excision, each cleared under the microscope and sutured, before an afternoon of general derm and cosmetics (6.7×).
- A New York derm's day runs from laser hair removal and Botox to keloid injections and a scalp cyst excision done "in record time, 10-15 minutes max" (8.1×).
- The medical assistant does the intake, numbing, scheduling and training, while the doctor "just" diagnoses (a skit, 7.7×).
- Ontario ending "rental" medical directors will close nurse-run clinics but make aesthetics safer, because a doctor must now meet every patient.
- A cosmetic dermatologist "of course" travels the world for the best lasers, and his wife is his billboard (self-parody, 3.7×).
- An independent California cosmetic dermatologist earns about $152 an hour with 12 weeks off (a salary channel, 0.87×).

## How it sounds

Doctors front 64% of it and brands 17%, and the voice is overwhelmingly chatty (57% casual-friend) with some humor (26%) and a luxury sheen (12%); there is almost nothing clinical. The top two videos open by stating the job in the first line, either as a question the viewer half-wonders ("what does a dermatologist even do?") or as a credential ("why I became triple board certified"). The many silent vlogs set to music, with no line at all, sit near the bottom.

Three opening lines that worked:

> “What does a dermatologist even do?” (@dr.mamina, 80×)
> “Let's talk about why I became a triple board certified dermatologist.” (@dr.mamina, 25×)
> “a day in my life as a dermatologist in New York City” (@toralvaidyamd, 13×)

## What the audience says back

Nothing, in this data: none of these videos has comment data recorded, and none of the 47 viewer questions from run 1 is about the practice itself. What the claims imply is that people don't know what a dermatologist does beyond Botox, are curious about the money and the hours, and, in the Ontario story, are starting to ask who is actually behind the needle at a clinic with a doctor's name on the door.

## Ostad's angle

- **Maps to:** the whole practice rather than one service: Mohs surgery and Mohs reconstruction (no Ostad TV video shows either), the AAAASF-certified surgical center, the Lexington Avenue office, EXOMIND and IV therapy, the out-of-town patient arrangement with The Surrey, and his own skincare line.
- **Only he can say:** "Why I became triple board certified" is literally his credential, and his version has NYU, Mass General, a UCLA Mohs fellowship and 20+ years in practice behind it. A senior surgeon's Upper East Side day (a Mohs case read under his own microscope, a reconstruction, then cosmetic patients, then teaching NYU residents) is a different film from the young-resident vlogs that dominate this theme. And as a physician-owned practice, he can take the "who is actually doing your injection" question head on.
- **The trap:** The lifestyle vlog (espresso, Pilates, galas, Sephora runs), staff lip-sync skits, salary talk, and silent luxury office tours set to music, which here score as low as 0.07× to 0.27×. His EXOMIND posts already do better than anything else he posts, but if every practice video is a device announcement it reads as a press release, not a look inside.
