# What a derm actually gets done

This theme is the insider's menu. Doctors rate treatments out of ten, confess what they have had done ("I'm almost 60"), and list what they would never get, never do on their own daughter, or never spend money on. The fights are specific: threads hold a month at most, Ultherapy and EMFACE leave patients underwhelmed, Morpheus8 eats fat pads, yet another derm credits Ultherapy for ten years of lifted eyelids. Underneath runs a skincare evidence ladder: sunscreen does most of the work, retinoids are the only proven topical, glow serums have no wrinkle data. People watch because it feels like getting a straight answer from someone who knows.

## The numbers

44 videos, 5% of the niche. 11% of them are winners and the median one does 0.88× its creator's usual (0.94× for the makeable ones); 77% (34 videos) are ones Dr. Ostad could make himself.

## What people claim

- Broad-spectrum sunscreen does 80% of anti-aging work, retinoids are the only topical proven to build collagen, and you do not need a ten-step routine.
- No skincare product can smooth, tighten pores and fade spots like a retinoid, and cheap drugstore ones are fine.
- Popular glow serums score close to zero on anti-aging evidence, while clinically tested peptide serums earn 10 out of 10 (a doctor's ranking, 50× usual).
- A suture can't hold tissue up for longer than a month, so threads are too good to be true.
- A surgeon would never get neck liposuction, under-eye Botox, filler between the eyes, a skin-only mini facelift or threads.
- Ultherapy, HIFU and EMFACE leave "a complete wake of unsatisfied and underwhelmed patients"; don't use a non-surgical tool for a surgical problem.
- Ultherapy lifted one derm's hooded eyes and jawline from 2014 to 2024, and works best spaced every two years.
- Skip RF microneedling and PDO threads; do Sculptra, plain microneedling and Sofwave instead.
- Preventative neuromodulators, including in the lower face, are the non-negotiable first step of an anti-aging plan.
- An aesthetic doctor won't give her 20-year-old daughter more than 1 cc of lip filler, threads or cheek filler (7.8× usual).

## How it sounds

Doctors carry this theme: they are 68% of the speakers, with a handful of influencers and nurse injectors. The tone is contrarian (39%) and clinical (30%), friendly rather than salesy. The winning first lines give a verdict straight away: a score, a hill to die on, an age, or a promise to compress years of evidence into a minute.

## What the audience says back

None of the videos in this theme came with recorded comments. The run 1 viewer questions show the demand clearly, though: "Can you make a video on retinol vs tretinoin?" (1,156 likes), "Which retinoid are you using? Please recommend something budget friendly." (1,116), "Do you use retinol every day?" (600) and "Can you please do one for in your 30s?" (101). People want the doctor's own routine and a list for their own decade, not a general lecture.

## Ostad's angle

- **Maps to:** the whole menu: Botox and Jeuveau, fillers, Sculptra, Fraxel, Morpheus8, Ultherapy, thread lifts, PRFM, Clear + Brilliant, plus his Ret Nia retinoid serums, Sun Shade sunscreens and the Anti-aging Regimen Set. His blog has "The Ultimate Guide to Anti-Aging for Each Decade" and "Botox in Your 30s, 40s & 50s", and he offers a free skincare evaluation.
- **Only he can say:** Nearly every "what I get done" video here is a woman (an injector at 54, a derm near 60). A male derm in his 50s with 20+ years on Park Avenue saying what he does on his own face speaks to his men's lane and is rarely seen. As a Mohs surgeon who has looked at skin under the microscope for over 25 years, "sunscreen first" is not a slogan from him. And because he does both threads and surgery, his "what I'd never get" list will differ from the surgeons and derms here, and he can explain why.
- **The trap:** Most of the "skip this" lists here name things he sells: threads, RF microneedling, Morpheus8, Ultherapy, EMFACE. A ranking where all of his services score 10 out of 10, or a "best retinoids" video that only leads to his $150 serum, would read as an ad. The other trap is the drugstore product haul, which belongs to younger creators.
