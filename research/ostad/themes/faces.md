# Faces: filler, fat and Ozempic face

This theme is about volume: who has too much, who lost it, and what fixing it does to you. The loudest strand is Ozempic face, with celebrity before-and-afters and doctors arguing whether it is the drug or just fast weight loss. Next is buccal fat removal, where derms warn that facial fat is what keeps you young and surgeons say a careful trim won't cause regret. Then the filler backlash: migration, the "filler mustache", an MRI of filler that spread. Sculptra and SkinVive are pitched as the subtler answer, while looksmaxxers try bone smashing and jaw balls. People watch because they are afraid of looking older, or looking done.

## The numbers

93 videos, 10% of the niche. 14% of them are winners and the median one does 1.07× its creator's usual; 66% (61 videos) are ones Dr. Ostad could make himself.

## What people claim

- Ozempic face isn't caused by semaglutide itself; it is what happens to anyone who loses a lot of weight very fast.
- GLP-1 weight loss thins the fat pads around the eyes and upper cheeks, unmasking hollows and tear troughs that were always there.
- People with fat in their face age the best, so removing buccal fat young takes away what aging will strip for free (a cosmetic derm, 4.9× usual).
- Buccal fat doesn't shrink with age like other fat, so judicious removal won't cause regret and can help in middle age too.
- An MRI of a 33-year-old who had 12 cc of filler over six years measured close to 28 cc, spread across the face.
- Lip filler migration causes a "filler mustache"; stop adding filler, wait out the swelling, then dissolve it.
- Cross-linked filler does not simply dissolve with hyaluronidase; it migrates, persists and can be encapsulated.
- Sculptra is not a filler: it triggers your own collagen over weeks to months and lasts two to three years.
- SkinVive is not a filler either: tiny droplets of hyaluronic acid hydrate the skin without changing your features.
- Face exercises, jaw balls and bone smashing can't add volume or reshape bone; the muscles and bones don't work that way.

## What the audience says back

Comments here are scarce: the one clip with recorded reactions is a looksmaxxing jawline joke, where viewers reply "it's genetics" and "it's over" and one warns that thumb-pulling is dangerous. The sharpest viewer voice is a run 1 comment, "are we fucking normalizing taking ozempic for non-essential purposes?", with 1,657 likes. So the audience is judging GLP-1 use as much as asking about fixes, and the claims show what worries them: looking older after weight loss, looking overfilled, and regretting something permanent.
