# What is this on my skin?

These videos give a bump, rash or patch a name, and just as often say what it isn't. Sudden forehead bumps that are yeast, not acne. Light patches that are neither a yeast infection nor vitiligo. Black dots on the nose that aren't blackheads. Pores that don't open and close. Rosacea is the biggest single topic, and the line everyone repeats is that it is controlled, never cured. Underneath runs a quieter fear: the pimple that won't heal, the "scar" that turned out to be basal cell carcinoma. People watch because a doctor names their thing in thirty seconds, and the name either calms them or sends them to book.

## The numbers

40 videos, 4% of the niche. 18% of them are winners and the median one does 0.96× its creator's usual (the same 0.96× for the ones he could make), so the typical video lands at par while the top six run from 84× to 201×; 85% are videos Dr. Ostad could make himself.

## What people claim

- Small red bumps that appear suddenly on the forehead and sides of the face may be yeast folliculitis, not acne (the theme's top video, 201×).
- Demodex mites live in everyone's pores, come out at night to mate, can't poop, and play a role in rosacea (149×).
- Light patches on the chest or back that don't respond to antifungal creams may be progressive macular hypomelanosis; "you could have the wrong diagnosis" (84×).
- A facial lump dismissed as a scar and lasered turned out, at biopsy, to be basal cell carcinoma that needed Mohs (a patient's story, 183×).
- A scaly red spot that "comes and goes" can be an actinic keratosis turning into basal cell, visible under the dermatoscope (20×).
- A pimple that grows for weeks, bleeds, or hasn't healed in six weeks is a red flag for skin cancer.
- Rosacea is a chronic inflammatory disease, not "sensitive skin" or poor hygiene; alcohol is a trigger, not the cause, and it is managed, never cured.
- Pores aren't doors: steam doesn't open them and cold water doesn't close them.
- The tiny black dots on the nose are often trapped vellus hairs (trichostasis spinulosa), not blackheads, and scraping blackheads makes pores and pigment worse.
- A rash is an emergency if it involves the eyes, mouth or genitals, blisters or sloughs, swells the face, comes with fever, or doesn't fade under a pressed glass.

## How it sounds

Doctors carry this theme (75% of speakers), and it is mostly clinical (58%) with a real streak of humor (25%); patients and influencers show up mainly to describe living with rosacea. The winning first line either names the thing on screen or flatly denies what you assumed it was ("this is not X"), so the viewer is checking their own skin before the second sentence. Contrarian claims (22%) and questions (18%) are the most common hooks.

Three opening lines that worked:

> “If you get these little red bumps that come up all of a sudden” (@skinbydrazi, 201×)
> “A boil doesn't suddenly appear.” (@healthinseconds6, 144×)
> “This is not a yeast infection, and it's not vitiligo either.” (@drderrickphillips, 84×)

## What the audience says back

The single most-liked viewer comment in the whole niche lives here: a joke that demodex "are having demosex on my face every night" (59,782 likes), and viewers of that video joked in disbelief but were oddly comforted that the mites don't poop. Under the basal cell story, survivors shared their own melanoma and SCC stories and urged second opinions, while one asked why Mohs is needed for a basal cell; another commenter elsewhere wrote "It's almost seeming like a scam now with Mohs surgery." The rest are short and anxious: "Should I be worried doc", "should I be worried this is in my foot?", "What test catch this?"

## Ostad's angle

- **Maps to:** Cutera Genesis and Excel V for rosacea redness and broken vessels; Mohs surgery and skin cancer reconstruction; mole removal; PDT for actinic keratosis; AviClear and the acne page; the Ethnic Skin Tones page (DPN, keloids, PIH), where DPN and seborrheic keratosis removal have no posts or videos yet. His own two videos here (milia, 3,752 views; rosacea, 227 views) are flat explainers without the "this is not X" opening.
- **Only he can say:** He is the Mohs surgeon who reads the slide. He can take the story of the "scar" that was cancer and the comment calling Mohs a scam, and answer both as the person who does the surgery and teaches it to NYU residents, and he can show what a basal cell looks like before and after he clears it. With a skin-of-colour page built on the Monk scale, he can do the "this looks different on darker skin" version (DPN vs. moles, keloid vs. scar) that almost no one in this theme does.
- **The trap:** Turning into a general-derm diagnosis feed (HIV rashes, boils, plantar warts, the meningitis glass test) that sends viewers to urgent care rather than a cosmetic practice on the Upper East Side; diagnosing a stranger's photo on camera; or a rosacea "best products" roundup that sells someone else's creams.
