# Lasers and the healing diary

This theme is the recovery, not the laser. The videos that travel are day-by-day diaries: Fraxel's brown dots darkening on day three and flaking off by day eight, a CO2 face that looks like “Freddy Krueger” but doesn't hurt, a tattoo frosting white on the first zap. Around them sit the arguments: whether laser should be the first thing you try for acne scars, whether Black skin can be lasered without burns, whether Fraxel was a waste of money, and who should be holding the handpiece. People watch to find out how bad the week after will look and whether it was worth it.

## What people claim

- CO2 resurfacing tightens skin and clears pigment, pores and scars, including body scars like C-sections; “the journey may not be pretty, but the results are absolutely worth it.”
- The snap of laser tattoo removal is a photoacoustic effect: light hits so hard it makes a sound wave and shatters the ink for the body to clear.
- After Fraxel, the marks darken around day three (“kind of look like freckles”), mostly fade by day eight, and pores and ice-pick scars look smaller by day ten.
- CO2 recovery looks frightening but isn't painful; one patient planned a month off and was healed in about a week.
- Acne scars respond so well to fractional CO2 that laser should be the first thing you try, not the last.
- Alexandrite lasers will very likely burn Black skin; Nd:YAG and the right operator make hair removal safe.
- Non-ablative Fraxel didn't just improve appearance: years of patient data showed it cut common skin cancers by about 50%.
- CO2 done too young or too hard gives a waxy look and pigment problems; it's for perioral lines in your fifties and sixties.
- Before paying thousands for a laser, ask what is being treated; if the answer is the name of the machine, walk away. Fall in love with a provider, not a machine.
- A CO2 laser left one patient with burn scars because the provider dismissed her pain and swelling; aftercare follow-up matters as much as the device.
- Fraxel took six days to heal and gave one patient nothing visible, so “I wasted money.”

Very few comments were captured for this theme; the only reading is on a first-session tattoo removal clip, where viewers say it triggers trypophobia and is agony to watch. The run-1 questions fill in the rest and they are all about the week after: “does it hurt?” (254 likes), “How long is recovery?”, “How soon can you wear foundation after each session?”, “How many sessions” and “What if your skin scars super easily”. The claims point at the same worries: pain, downtime, burns on darker skin, and paying thousands for nothing.
