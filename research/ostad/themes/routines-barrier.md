# Routines, the skin barrier and glass skin

The biggest theme in the niche is a tug of war between more and less. On one side: glass-skin routines, K-beauty steps, PDRN creams, skin cycling and 25-product mornings, much of it sponsored. On the other: dermatologists showing a real routine of wash, retinoid, done, and reacting with horror as a creator's face reddens under 20 products. The damaged barrier is the price of overdoing it, and the fixes are boring: stop actives, moisturize damp skin, petroleum jelly or zinc cream overnight. People watch for the glow, out of fear they've wrecked their skin, and to see a doctor roast a ridiculous routine.

## The numbers

107 videos, 12% of the niche. 18% of them are winners and the median one does 1.29× its creator's usual (1.08× for the ones he could make); 53% (57 videos) are videos Dr. Ostad could make himself.

## What people claim

- Skin cycling (an exfoliant night, a retinoid night, then two recovery nights) gives skin that looks like an Instagram filter.
- Skin cycling underdoses the exfoliant: once a week is barely more than splashing water on your face, and the whole idea is common sense packaged as marketing.
- A dermatologist's real routine is wash and retinoid at night, wash in the morning, and you don't need to buy into the industry.
- Nine times out of ten people moisturize wrong: put it on right after washing, while the skin is still damp.
- Twenty products at once will inflame your barrier; stick to three or four hydrating basics and fix one problem at a time.
- When the barrier is damaged, stop every active, retinoids included, until it heals.
- Zinc oxide diaper cream on the face overnight heals a damaged barrier fast; so does a pea-size amount of Vaseline patted on.
- You can get smoother skin with no acids or retinoids at all, just plain moisturizer or petroleum jelly.
- K-pop idols' glass skin comes from daily barrier care with PDRN cream.
- Celebrities who say "water and sleep" are hiding the clinic treatments behind their glow.
- A simple cleanse, sunscreen and retinoid routine in your twenties pays off for decades.

## How it sounds

It's split almost evenly between doctors (50%) and influencers (43%), and the tone is casual (40% casual-friend, 17% humor). Influencers sell steps and products (21% salesy, 14% sponsored). The doctors who win do the opposite: they open with "I'm a dermatologist" and then take things away, or they use the "Hi, dermatologist here" reaction format to mock a maximalist routine. The winning first line sets up a contradiction between what TikTok shows and what a doctor actually does.

Three opening lines that worked:

> “I'm a dermatologist, and here's my real nightly routine.” (@drgazy, 52× usual)
> “Nine times out of 10, I find that people are moisturizing wrong.” (@dr.mamina, 92× usual)
> “Okay, so this is way too many products.” (@dermdocmalik, 29× usual)

## What the audience says back

The comments here are detailed and practical. Under the minimalist derm routine, viewers are startled by a retinoid with no moisturizer, question skipping daily sunscreen on a retinoid, ask for a budget retinoid and cheer "less is more". Under the barrier-signs video they ask how to tell purging from a bad product, how long healing takes, and share their own perioral dermatitis cases. Run 1's questions repeat it: how long does a barrier take to heal, should I still wear sunscreen when my barrier is damaged, why does my face feel tight however much I moisturize, and routines for teens and for your 30s.

## Ostad's angle

- **Maps to:** Pure Creamy Cleanser (for rosacea and post-procedure skin), Hyla Complex (barrier moisturizer), Nia-C+ (niacinamide and ceramides), the Hyaluronic Peptide Serum, the Rosacea/Redness and Anti-aging Regimen Sets, the Sun Shade sunscreens and the free skincare evaluation; in the office, DiamondGlow, HydraFacial, microinfusion and SKINVIVE (the honest "glass skin" injectable, "a healthy, dewy-looking aspect").
- **Only he can say:** he appeared in the WSJ's "Is Your Antiaging Routine Making Your Skin Look Worse?" (Dec 2025), and "less is more" is already an Ostad TV title, so the subtract-don't-add voice is his by record. Every derm routine here belongs to a woman in her 20s to 40s; a Park Avenue dermatologist in his 50s showing what he actually puts on his face is an empty lane. And because he lasers and resurfaces skin, he has seen what a truly broken barrier looks like after CO2, and can tell viewers when "barrier damage" is just dryness.
- **The trap:** his six existing videos in this theme are near-silent DiamondGlow and AquaGold facial clips set to music, pulling 279 to 1,055 views. That is the spa ASMR genre, where an esthetician's dermaplaning clip does 10.9M views and a doctor brings nothing extra. A ten-step routine, a sponsored PDRN cream or a skin-cycling copy would make him one more voice on the "more" side.
