# Acne and acne scars

This theme splits in two. The first half is scars: influencers post silent before/afters crediting a serum or scar sheets, a cartoon skit promises glycolic acid and rosehip oil will "erase" pitted scars, and doctors answer with one correction: red and brown spots are marks that fade, only dents are true scars, and nothing works while you are still breaking out. The second half is stubborn adult and hormonal acne: stop washing with soap, check for PCOS, Accutane versus a vitamin A supplement. People watch because they want their old skin back and are tired of products that promised it.

## The numbers

28 videos, 3% of the niche. 25% of them are winners and the median one does 1.37× its creator's usual (1.03× for the ones he could make); 61% (17 videos) are videos Dr. Ostad could make himself.

## What people claim

- Red or dark spots after a pimple are marks that fade on their own; only the little dents are true (atrophic) scars.
- Popping pimples pushes the gunk deeper and causes the scarring.
- You can't fix scars while you are still breaking out; control the acne first, then treat the dents.
- There is no single best scar treatment: subcision for rolling scars, TCA cross for ice pick, RF microneedling or CO2 for texture, and 50 to 80% improvement is a realistic goal, not perfection.
- Glycolic acid, vitamin C and rosehip oil used nightly will erase deep pitted scars at home.
- A serum, or a pack of scar sheets, removed my acne marks and scars.
- For hormonal acne, stop washing with soap: it strips the barrier and makes breakouts worse.
- Adult women with acne should see a dermatologist because the cause can be PCOS.
- Don't let anyone inject filler or a biostimulator into skin with active acne; it can trap bacteria.
- A popular acne supplement contains about twice the adult upper limit of vitamin A, which makes supervised Accutane the safer choice.

## How it sounds

Doctors carry slightly more than half of it (54%) and influencers about a third (36%). The tone is mostly clinical (36%) with a heavy salesy streak (25%) and real confession (21%) from people who have tried everything. The biggest view counts go to influencer before/after reveals with no useful speech, but the doctor videos that win open with the exact question the viewer is already asking, then reframe it in the next sentence ("are they actual scars or just marks?").

## What the audience says back

Comments are thin here. Under the 1,274× serum video, people trade how often to apply it and purging tips, some say it made their skin worse, and skeptics say no serum beats a laser. The loudest signal is two New York women posting their own videos begging for an NYC acne dermatologist who "really cares" after Accutane and years of spironolactone stopped working. Run 1's viewer questions add the same worries: what if your skin scars easily, does this work on brown skin prone to hyperpigmentation, and what if everything except water breaks you out.
