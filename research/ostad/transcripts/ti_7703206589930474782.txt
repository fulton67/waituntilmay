Every year when November rolls around, I get tons of messages about how to optimise your residency application. I've been in the game for nearly a decade. I started medical school in 2015. I matched around COVID, then finished my intern year and dermatology residency, and I've been an attending for a few years. I've been a medical student on the other side, but I've also sat on admissions committees and tutored other students going through the same process. So if you wanna listen to me, these are my top three tips that I give every single one of my students and why I think I was successful in terms of the match. Number one, mentorship is the most important thing. Think about it very intuitively here. A mentor will give you a sense of the field, but the most important thing is that they're going to advocate for you. They can put you in touch with colleagues for rotations or research. They have a sense of what different programs entail, community versus academic. And hopefully they will pick up the phone when someone calls about who you are as an applicant. Mentorship is the most important thing, I will say. Absolutely, hands down. Especially when it comes to dermatology. Second thing I'll say is just commit to something. I would much rather take a student who has shown an interest long term in something, even if they ended up changing their mind later, rather than bouncing around many different fields and doing one project here or there. So, for example, this could be starting a club, working with a non profit, doing research. Show that you are doing something over time and building yourself within that role. It's going to pay off even if you don't end up doing that field, because it just shows that you can get the job done. Tip No. 3. Do something different. So, yes, I had the grades, I had the research. Like, not to brag, but I had, like, 25 publications going into residency applications. But guess what came up more than any of those things. Nothing to do with medicine. It was actually about fashion design. So before I started medical school, I actually studied fashion design and ended up getting a degree in it. This came up way, way more than my research or my grades because it was different, and it showed that I could use a different part of my brain and commit to something. Hands down, this came up on every single interview I went on. Those are my big three. Mentorship, commit to something, and do something different.
