After more than 30 years of treating patients, one of the things I've come to believe very strongly about is that in aesthetics, less is often more. When a patient comes into my office, my goal isn't to change the way they look. It's to understand what makes their face uniquely theirs and to preserve it. Every face has its own proportions, expressions, asymmetries, and characteristics. Those aren't necessarily things we need to correct. They are part of what makes someone recognizable. So when I assess a patient, I don't start by asking, what can I inject? I ask, what does this patient actually need? And sometimes the answer is very little. Sometimes it's nothing at all. I think we have reached an interesting point in aesthetic medicine where we have more tools available to us than ever before. But just because we can do something doesn't necessarily mean we should. For me, a successful treatment shouldn't announce itself. I don't want someone to walk into a room and have people think she had filler or he had something done. I want people to think, you look great. Maybe they can't even identify why. To me, that's the difference between changing someone's face and refining it. I'm not trying to eliminate every single line. I'm not trying to remove every sign of aging. And I'm certainly not trying to make every patient look the same. Aging to me is part of being human. And my roles to understand where a thoughtful intervention can make a meaningful difference without really taking away the character of the face. That's also why I tend to take really a gradual approach. We can always reassess. We can always enhance later further. We can always do more when it's appropriate. But restraint really gives us the ability to preserve balance, expression and proportion along the way. And after really decades of working with the face, I have developed a tremendous respect for its complexity and really perhaps an even greater respect for knowing when to leave something alone. My philosophy is really simple. Don't chase perfection. Preserve the individual. Use the right treatment in the right place for the right patient and only as much as you truly need. Because ultimately, the best aesthetic work shouldn't make you look like someone else. It should allow you to look like the best version of yourself.
