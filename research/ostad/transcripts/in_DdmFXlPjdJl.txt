Hi everyone. I want to talk to you about the issues with regards to fillers and why do so many plastic surgeons suddenly seem to hate filler. They're not completely wrong about the problem, but I think they're blaming the wrong thing. The problem isn't really the filler, it's how the filler has been used. For years, we saw an era of static medicine where almost every sign of aging was treated by adding more volume, bigger cheeks, sharper jaw lines, fuller lips, filling every hollow, but filler was never really designed to fix everything. If the real problem is significant skin laxity or tissue descent, adding more filler isn't necessarily going to help too much. So sometimes the right answer is really a laser skin tightening treatment. Sometimes it's truly a facelift surgery, but sometimes it's absolutely the filler is needed. So as both a dermatologist and a surgeon, I use all these modalities and I don't believe that filler or surgery should be competing with each other. Filler can be incredibly effective when small amounts are placed strategically to restore support, improve proportion, and really subtly improve the face. So the mistake is treating filler like a commodity, one syringe for your cheek, two for your jawline, and faces don't really come in syringes, and the treatment should really follow the anatomy, not the trend. And sometimes the best filler treatment I can give a patient is telling them they don't need filler at all, because the future of really natural-looking aesthetics isn't filler versus surgery, it's better judgment.
